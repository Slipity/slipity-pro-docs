Every screen has a role:

- **Audience screens** show the service: lyrics, scripture, media and cameras.
- **Stage screens** show the people on the platform a [stage layout](/presenting/stage-screens): what's next, the time, notes and chords.

Set them up in **Settings › Screens**.

## Projectors and TVs

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connect the display">Over HDMI, USB-C or an adapter. It appears under **Hardware** in **Settings › Screens**.</Step>
  <Step title="Add a screen">Add an audience or stage screen and choose the display for it.</Step>
  <Step title="Check which is which">**Identify Screens** puts each screen's name on its display.</Step>
</Steps>

Slipity Pro remembers a display by the display itself, not the port it's in, so it comes back to the same job whenever it's connected — and it won't guess between two identical projectors without asking.

On the Mac, **⌥⌘1** and **⌥⌘2** turn the audience and stage screens on and off. They work even when an output covers the screen you're working at. **⇧⌘R** (**Refresh Displays**) looks for displays again.

## Making it look right

| Setting | What it's for |
|---|---|
| **Test Pattern** | Focus and line up a projector. |
| **Corner Pin** | Square up a picture from a projector mounted at an angle. |
| **Edge Blend** | Blend two or more projectors into one wide picture. |
| **Alpha Key** | Send a key signal for a video switcher, so the words can go over a camera picture. |
| **Screen Color** | Fine-tune a display's colour to match the others. |
| **2 × 2 Quadrant** | Split one signal four ways, for a device that feeds four screens. |
| **TV Wall** | Make several displays one picture. |
| **Output Target** | Choose whether a screen shows the full output or a Look made for it. |

## Slide shape

Slides are 16:9 (1920 × 1080) to start. If your main screen is another shape, such as a 4:3 projector or a wide wall of blended projectors, make the slides that shape so they fill it:

1. Open **Settings › Screens** and choose your main audience screen.
2. Under **Slides**, click **Make Slides This Shape**. Slipity Pro works out the screen's shape from its displays, less the overlap where they blend.

Or choose a shape from **Slide Shape**: 16:9, 16:10, 4:3, 21:9 or 32:9.

The slide shape is kept with your library and syncs, so every device makes the same slides. Slides stay 1080 high, so words keep their size and the slides grow or shrink at the sides. Text boxes widen and their lines rewrap; background pictures and films keep their **Fit** or **Fill**.

**Slide Fit** says what a screen does when it's a different shape from the slides:

| Slide Fit | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Fit** | All of each slide, as large as it will go, with black bars where the screen is left over. |
| **Fill** | The screen covered, with the slide's edges cut off. |
| **Stretch** | The slide pulled to the screen's shape, out of proportion. |

New screens use **Fit**. Screens set up before Slide Fit existed keep stretching, as they always did, until you choose otherwise. The preview shows the slides at their shape, with bars where the preview is a different shape.

Everything else follows the slide shape too: the slide grid, the editor, theme previews, the Remote, printing and exporting pictures and films. When you change the shape, Slipity Pro offers to keep pictures, shapes and QR codes in proportion. **Adjust** keeps each one its size and proportions, about the same center, so a circle stays a circle; anything across the whole width stays across it. **Don't Adjust** leaves them as a share of the slide, stretched or squeezed with it. Words rewrap either way. Adjusting changes every slide it touches, so they sync to your other devices again. Check slides with words side by side afterward.

In the editor, slides of another shape than 16:9 show a dashed **16:9** box: the part a 16:9 screen still shows with **Fill**. Keep what matters inside it if your slides also go to 16:9 screens, such as a lobby TV or LightLoop. LightLoop's Apple TVs are 16:9, so slides sent there are laid out at 16:9.

## Apple TV

Install the free **Slipity Screen** app on an Apple TV on the same network as your Mac, iPad or iPhone.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Slipity Screen on the TV">It waits to be found.</Step>
  <Step title="Choose the Apple TV">In **Settings › Screens**, choose it as a screen's display.</Step>
  <Step title="Type the code">The TV shows a code. Type it in to pair. Pairing lets this device drive it, and takes over from any Mac driving it now.</Step>
</Steps>

The Apple TV draws the slides itself, at its own refresh, from the same descriptions your Mac draws its own screens from — so a fade is as smooth as on a projector, not a video of one. The running order is sent ahead, so a slide change only sends a few kilobytes.

## AirPlay and external displays on iPad and iPhone

A display connected to iPad or iPhone — by cable, or with AirPlay — can be an audience or stage screen, so a small group can run from a phone and a TV.
