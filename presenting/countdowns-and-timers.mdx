A **timer** is a layer that shows a clock instead of words you typed. Put one on a slide, in a countdown, or on a stage layout.

## What it counts

| Mode | For |
|---|---|
| **Countdown to Time** | Down to a time of day — doors at 10:30, the service at 11. Choose **Any Day** for one that's the same every week. |
| **Countdown Timer** | Down from a length — five minutes of notices, thirty seconds of a bumper. |
| **Elapsed Time** | Up from the moment it was put on the screen — how long the sermon has been going. |

Choose how it's written, from **SS** and **M:SS** up to hours, days, frames and timecode.

### Once the time has passed

A countdown to a time that goes up after that time is already over: it hides at once, or stays at zero if it isn't set to hide. With a day chosen, that lasts until the same day comes round next week — right for a weekly service, where the slide shown after it shouldn't start counting to next Sunday.

Turn on **Count to Next Once Passed** for one that should count to the next one instead: tomorrow's time, or with a day chosen, next week's. A "doors open at 7" countdown used every evening is the one to turn it on for.

## When it gets to zero

**On Complete** in the timer's settings can do several things at once — for the pre-service countdown, say, hide itself, clear the music and go straight into the service.

| Choice | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Hide Countdown** | The timer takes itself off; what's behind it stays. Turned off, the digits stay at zero until something else goes up. |
| **Go to Next Slide** | On to the next slide in the running order. |
| **Go to Playlist** | Straight into a playlist — the service itself, from the pre-service countdown. |
| **Clear** | Takes any of the other layers off: Audio, Overlays, Slides, Media or Video Input. Choose as many as you like. |
| **Run Macro** | Runs one or more of your [macros](/presenting/macros): the Look, stage layout and lighting cue for the start of the service, all at once. |

Go to Next Slide and Go to Playlist are one or the other. A timer on a stage layout can only hide or stay at zero.

A timer can also carry on past zero, counting the other way — useful on a stage screen, so the speaker knows how far over they are.

## Countdowns

A **countdown** is a timer with a design of its own, made in the **Countdown Editor**, that shows on the Countdown layer, over the slides and under any overlay, so an overlay that frames the screen frames the countdown too. Start one:

- from a slide, with **Add Action › Show Countdown**,
- from a remote, MIDI or OSC,
- or with Siri and Shortcuts: **Start Countdown** and **Stop Countdown**.

**Clear Countdown** takes it off.
