## Add a camera

Connect a camera or capture card, then add it in **Settings › Inputs** under **Video Inputs**. Give it a name, and choose how it **Fits** the screen.

A camera can go on the screens as:

- the **Video Input** layer, from a slide (**Add Action › Show Live Video**) or the Media panel,
- a **layer on a slide** — the preacher in a box beside the scripture,
- a **background** behind the words.

## Framing

Each camera has a **framing**:

| Framing | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Off** | The picture as it comes. |
| **Fixed** | Zoomed and placed by hand, and left there. |
| **Follow** | Zoomed in on a person and moved with them — a camera on a tripod made to pan and zoom like a robotic one. |

For **Follow**, choose the shot — **Wide** (head to foot), **Medium** (waist up) or **Close** (head and shoulders) — and who to follow: the **Most Prominent Person**, or the **Person I Tap** in the viewfinder.

A camera layer on a slide can have its own framing, so the same camera can be wide behind the words and close in a box beside them.

## Changing framing during a service

Change a camera's framing from the **Screens** menu on the Mac, the **•••** menu on iPad and iPhone, a slide (**Add Action › Camera Framing**), a remote, or Siri and Shortcuts (**Set Camera Framing**). The change stays, as a robotic camera stays where it was last sent.

## iPhone Duo's back screen

On iPhone Duo, **Back Screen Camera** shows your slides on the screen on the back of the phone, with a viewfinder in a corner of yours so you can see who's in front of it.
